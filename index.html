Chapter 1 preview

The Man I Was

This is the chapter that sells the narrator. It starts in history, moves into childhood, then reveals the emotional shutdown that makes the later life review devastating instead of sentimental.

“The cause had failed. The discipline survived.”

The reader magnet here is the reversal: the book is not asking readers to trust an easy believer. It introduces a man who was sealed, strategic, and dangerous to others, then makes the death event matter because that man is forced to become a witness against himself.

Selected sample

The inherited commandment

My mother was born in 1941, inside the machinery of the Third Reich, to parents who believed in it.

The cause had failed. The discipline survived.

Ideas do not always die when their governments do. Sometimes they shrink down into kitchen rules, into the tone of a father’s voice, into the way a mother stiffens when a child cries. Sometimes history stops wearing a uniform and learns to live in the body.

Out of that house, out of that decade, out of that inheritance, my mother carried one polished sentence into the rest of her life:

Emotion is weakness, and weakness gets used.

Open the deeper hook

The crying stopped. Then something underneath the crying stopped too.

You cannot close the door on grief and leave joy moving freely through the house. Wonder goes in with it. Awe goes in with it. Love waits outside for a while, then stops knocking.

The question is not whether a man like that deserved a second chance. He did not. The question is what it means that he got one anyway.

Chapter 11 preview

Field Nights

This is the chapter that makes the website feel bigger. It gives readers a location, a method, a mystery, and a reason the paranormal material is not being treated like entertainment.

“Investigation is the wrong word. Search and rescue.”

The draw is cinematic but disciplined: a downtown Dallas hotel, a floor removed from booking, solo investigation rules, an unnamed protected source, and a room where the past still feels physically present.

Selected sample

The twenty-fourth floor

The Magnolia Hotel took a floor out of inventory.

Not closed for renovation. Not unfinished. The twenty-fourth floor is furnished and maintained like the floors above and below it. Housekeeping still goes there. The booking system does not.

Things moved. Objects appeared where no one had left them. Other things disappeared. Guests and staff heard noises with no source. Items fell from surfaces in empty rooms. More than one person reported the same feeling: being watched.

A full floor of a downtown Dallas hotel, held out of inventory after enough strange reports that someone decided the lost revenue was cheaper than whatever kept happening upstairs.

Open the field-night hook

A hotel floor in perfect readiness, with nobody on it, has a silence all its own. Doors in their ranks. Beds made behind them for guests who never come.

The room was occupied. I registered it the way you register someone in your peripheral vision who was not there when you sat down.

There are people in these buildings. Not attractions. Not content. People stranded on the wrong side of their own fear, some of them for lifetimes, waiting for someone to come through the door who knows the territory.
