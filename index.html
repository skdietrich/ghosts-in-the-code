What readers need to know first

This is not a comfort memoir, a ghost-hunting scrapbook, or an AI panic piece.

Ghosts in the Code is a witness record. A historian and systems investigator dies for five minutes, returns with a moral accounting he cannot dismiss, and follows the evidence trail into haunted rooms, tested mediumship, and the new industry teaching machines to speak in the voices of the dead.

The central question

What survives, what only imitates survival, and how can we tell the difference?

The book does not ask readers to choose belief or debunking on page one. It asks them to examine the witness, follow the record, and decide what the evidence can honestly carry.

Why trust the witness?

The book begins by cross-examining its own narrator.

Before the ambulance, before the void, before the medium and the hotel floor, the reader meets the man who had to be broken open to tell the truth. A child taught not to cry becomes an adult trained to read rooms, systems, motives, and weaknesses.

That is why the testimony matters. The book does not begin with belief. It begins with an audit.

Preview library

Five entry points into the book.

Each preview answers a different reader question: who is speaking, what happened, what the review means, why the field work matters, and why AI belongs in a book about the dead.

Prologue preview

The Wrong Witness

The book opens by refusing the usual bargain. It does not offer instant proof, instant comfort, or instant dismissal. It offers a record from a witness trained to distrust records.

“I do not ask you to believe me. I ask you to check my work.”

This tells readers the rules of the whole book: the testimony will be personal, but it will not be careless. The author is not selling certainty. He is disclosing what happened and asking the reader to examine the file.

What this preview tells the reader

The promise of the book

A death event. A life review. Field nights. A medium under limits. A history of machines that claimed to carry voices. And now AI, fluent enough to make grief feel answered.

The prologue gives the reader the frame: this is a vulnerability disclosure from both sides of the boundary.

Chapter 1 preview

The Man I Was

Chapter 1 tells readers why the witness matters. It starts in history, moves into childhood, and shows how emotional survival equipment became a sealed life.

“The cause had failed. The discipline survived.”

The chapter makes the later life review devastating because the reader understands what kind of man is being reviewed: observant, controlled, strategic, and almost impossible to comfort or deceive.

Selected sample

The inherited commandment

My mother was born in 1941, inside the machinery of the Third Reich, to parents who believed in it.

The cause had failed. The discipline survived.

Sometimes history stops wearing a uniform and learns to live in the body.

Emotion is weakness, and weakness gets used.

Read why it matters

The crying stopped. Then something underneath the crying stopped too.

You cannot close the door on grief and leave joy moving freely through the house. Wonder goes in with it. Awe goes in with it. Love waits outside for a while, then stops knocking.

The question is not whether a man like that deserved a second chance. He did not. The question is what it means that he got one anyway.

Chapter 3 preview

The Review

The life review is the book’s moral center. It is not a comforting slideshow. It is the unedited record of harm, intention, and consequence, experienced from inside the people who carried the cost.

“The review is not watched. It is felt.”

This chapter tells readers what is at stake. The question is no longer only whether consciousness survives. The question is what survives with us.

What this preview tells the reader

Not guilt. Accuracy.

The review weighs intention, not reputation. Kindness performed as marketing enters as marketing. Cruelty dressed as business enters as cruelty.

The chapter gives the book its severity: the afterlife question is inseparable from the moral record of the life that arrives there.

Chapter 11 preview

Field Nights

Chapter 11 gives readers the field record: a downtown Dallas hotel, a preserved twenty-fourth floor, a protected source, solo access, and a night that changes the author’s vocabulary from investigation to search and rescue.

“Investigation is the wrong word. Search and rescue.”

The chapter matters because it refuses to treat the paranormal as entertainment. If the account is true even in part, the question is not only what was detected. The question is who might still be afraid, and what responsibility the living have when they enter those rooms.

Selected sample

The twenty-fourth floor

The Magnolia Hotel took a floor out of inventory.

Not closed for renovation. Not unfinished. The twenty-fourth floor is furnished and maintained like the floors above and below it. Housekeeping still goes there. The booking system does not.

Things moved. Objects appeared where no one had left them. Other things disappeared. Guests and staff heard noises with no source. Items fell from surfaces in empty rooms. More than one person reported the same feeling: being watched.

Read the turn

There are people in these buildings. Not attractions. Not content. People stranded on the wrong side of their own fear, some of them for lifetimes, waiting for someone to come through the door who knows the territory.

That night lives in the witness log as an uncorroborated account. The book says so clearly. The honesty is part of the evidence culture, not a weakness in it.

Chapter 12 preview

The Digital Séance

The old séance table has become a server room. A voice built from data says the thing grief most needs to hear, and the danger is not that it lies. The danger is that it can sound sincere.

“The dead in the box are perfectly agreeable.”

This is where the AI side of the website becomes essential. The book does not ask only whether the dead can speak. It asks what happens when a machine can imitate the answer well enough to change a grieving person’s life.

What this preview tells the reader

The mirror problem

A living person can surprise you. A simulation can only recombine the archive it was given and the grief that trained it.

The chapter gives the modern danger its shape: not fraud in the old sense, but engineered comfort, delivered in a voice the bereaved already miss.

Reader FAQ

What kind of book is this?

Is this memoir, paranormal investigation, or AI ethics?

It is all three, held together as a witness record. The death event begins the book; the field work tests what followed; the AI chapters ask what happens when technology learns to imitate contact.

Is the book asking readers to believe everything?

No. The book distinguishes testimony, anomaly, evidence, and proof. It includes empty nights and limitations because the method matters as much as the mystery.

What is Cindy Kaza’s role?

Cindy Kaza is featured as an important mediumship presence in the story. The site presents her role respectfully while leaving the full context for the book.

Why does AI belong in a book about ghosts?

Because the newest machine is entering the oldest human wound. A simulation of the dead may be useful, comforting, or dangerous, but it must not be mistaken for the thing it imitates without careful testing.
